# Implemented product scope

Vela is an embodied patient experience and room-operations research application. Its first measurable hypothesis is that permitted belongings can be made accessible and rooms prepared with fewer non-clinical staff interruptions. No pilot has measured that hypothesis here.

The working software supports bedside requests, authoritative-fixture status answers, staff escalation records, room objects and temporal changes, eight desired-state templates, inspectable task planning and approval, safety refusal, verification, fleet capability inspection, synthetic correction recording and a research studio. Room-state templates describe zone membership and clear egress. They do not yet model every clinical workflow or semantic orientation/support-surface constraint.

Three signature demonstrations are implemented: a phone begins at a randomized synthetic location; staff prepare a three-object physio arrangement; an uncertain phone stops autonomy and leads to an operator-accepted synthetic correction episode. No person is touched, no medical object is moved, and no hospital message is actually sent.

Primary product measures are actual software task completion and escalation counts. Staff minutes saved, patient autonomy gain, physical grasp success and real-world reliability remain null. Corridor logistics is a supporting future skill. The proposed differentiator is patient-room goal-to-state transformation with verification and temporal memory; novelty is a hypothesis, not a proven market claim. See [competitive evidence](COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md).

The VLA registry includes cluttered personal-item retrieval, drawer families, bimanual bag stabilization, clean fabric research, room reset and abstention. These are research task definitions. Only a tiny synthetic state-feature behavior-cloning baseline has been trained. No image-conditioned or language-conditioned policy is competent on these tasks yet.
